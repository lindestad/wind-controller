# Pre-order review disposition — revision B.1

The substantive, locally actionable corrections from the [executed review](reports/preorder-review/REPORT.md) are implemented in B.1. The supported load is **four ARCTIC P14 Pro PST fans, one per board header**. Their PST expansion sockets remain unused. This preserves independent tach feedback and the existing current budget. No legacy fan, radio, universal-input or safety-rated emergency-stop requirement is added.

This is a corrected first-hardware design, not a claim that unmanufactured hardware has passed operational qualification. Production Zephyr firmware remains a separate implementation task. The old **$82.72** quote covers older inputs; B.1 has not been quoted or ordered.

## Decisions

| Review item | Engineering decision and resulting change |
|---|---|
| R-01: startup margin | Worth improving. C8 changes from 10 nF to **100 nF**, and new **R40 = 100 ohms** follows TI's recommendation for capacitors above 10 nF. The nominal output slew becomes approximately **364 ms**, reducing known reservoir charging current tenfold. Firmware must hold all PWM pins low for **750 ms after enabling**, then start individual fans sequentially, allowing each to establish tach before the next. Do not raise the 2.395 A nominal current limit. Exact standby/startup current and startup time remain first-board measurements. |
| R-02: supply/OVLO margin | The absence of an ARCTIC maximum-voltage guarantee is real missing information, not evidence that a normal 12 V supply damages the fan. The former broad 11.4–12.6 V controller target gave little nuisance-trip margin. Specify a regulated **12.0 V / at least 3 A** adapter with **11.70–12.30 V including normal ripple at the board**, and **at most 120 mV peak-to-peak ripple**. Keep R11/R12 and the existing approximately 12.91 V cutoff. This is an engineering operating target, not a newly discovered fan absolute maximum. Filtering still does not regulate DC. |
| R-03: direct tach-to-GPIO exposure | A hypothetical hidden pull-up is not proof of a fault in the P14 Pro PST. Nevertheless, the correction is inexpensive and removes an unnecessary dependency on undocumented fan internals. Replace R23/R28/R33/R38 with **Q6–Q9 common-gate MOSFET level shifters**. Gate connects to 3V3, source to MCU tach, drain to the fan tach pin. Move the pull-up to the MCU side and change it to **2.2k**. There is no direct resistor from raw tach to the MCU supply. **R19 = 1k** gives the logic rail a defined discharge/leakage load. This is voltage isolation, not galvanic isolation. |
| R-04: PWM gate-drive guarantee | Fix. Q2–Q9 use the exact **MCC SI2312-TP / C6488168**, specified at **1.8 V gate drive**, below the approximately 2.40 V conservative PWM gate bound. Pinout remains G1/S2/D3 and the SOT-23 footprint fits. The same purchased transistor serves PWM and tach, so no second transistor feeder type is introduced. Exact manufacturer datasheet and sourcing evidence are retained. |
| R-05: eFuse stencil | Fix the CAD prescription now. U4 keeps its copper lands and gets separate paste-only apertures following TI drawing **4225183/A**: two **0.28 × 1.06 mm** rounded windows per long power pad, 0.20 mm separation, and reduced rounded L-shaped corner apertures. The corner vertical arms are **0.225 × 0.650 mm**, centred at X = ±0.7125 mm; horizontal arms are **0.600 × 0.275 mm**. Corner radius 0.05 mm. Supply a **0.100 mm stencil** requirement. TI calls its example 82%/93%; the present square-corner copper polygons differ slightly in area, so verify actual exported aperture areas rather than claiming exact percentage equality. Supplier stencil/placement confirmation still happens at the assembly stage. |
| R-06: residual energy after reset | A real limitation, but not grounds for a second shutdown circuit in this wind accessory. A normally commanded stop must pull PWM low before disabling fan power. On abrupt USB loss/reset, PWM may release while the reservoir discharges; brief coasting or a twitch is allowed. Sustained powered running is not. Do not describe this as an instantaneous or safety-rated stop. The first-board test must record the actual residual behaviour. |
| R-07: thermal/transient qualification | Keep the power path and its wide traces. The revised USB/signal routing preserves the reviewed common power and USB data trace geometry. At the intended 1.4 A running load the previous modest DC-loss estimates remain applicable, but are not an enclosure temperature rating or a 3 A continuous load qualification. Keep radios disabled and measure wired/ROM/flash current and enclosure temperatures on the first boards. A complete validated fan, cable, silicon and enclosure model is unavailable; inventing one would not improve confidence. |
| R-08: firmware/watchdog | Fix the specification's overclaims. Require **500 ms valid-frame freshness**, stop-before-disable, bounded startup/fault handling, whole-system watchdog reset and no GPIO hold. CPU-only reset is insufficient. A hardware FLT-to-MCU connection or external watchdog is unnecessary for the stated product; do not claim instant fault classification. SimHub sends unchanged frames periodically at **10 Hz or faster**, with changes-only disabled. Production firmware is still not delivered by this hardware change. |
| R-09: 0.12 mm silk graphics | Cosmetic, but a cheap legitimate fabrication fix. Raise exposed graphical strokes to **at least 0.15 mm**, including local libraries. Enable 1.0 mm text height, 0.15 mm text stroke and silk gap, and 0.09 mm mask-to-neighbour-copper clearance rules. Also audit plotted graphics because KiCad text rules do not cover them. Preserve FAULT to the right of its LED, STATUS to the left, and TP5's clearance. |

## Other findings

- **Removed** R24/R29/R34/R39 and their optional USB-powered PWM pull-up routes. ARCTIC specifies internally sourced PWM signalling; the unused option added routing and a possible future back-powering mistake. No substitute pull-up is required by this fan model. Measure the normal fan/cable waveform before changing this decision.
- **Retained** the two tooling holes, four mounts, 70 × 64 mm outline, black mask and LINDESTAD silkscreen. Two tooling holes without component courtyards are a library bookkeeping issue; their actual hole/mask/copper clearances are audited.
- Legacy local-symbol footprint-filter warnings concern renamed library identifiers. They are not evidence of a wrong package. Exact pad mapping, copper, drills and assembly files are the meaningful checks.
- The missing U4 and USB-C 3D bodies remain visual omissions. Their physical lands, BOM and placements remain present. No fabricated model is used as mechanical evidence.
- RF antenna clearance is not redesigned for a wired-only controller. Radios must stay off. No arbitrary 24 V input survival, hot reverse-polarity survival or automatic OVLO recovery requirement is added.
- OVLO does not assert FLT; the red LED can remain dark during an overvoltage shutdown. The normal recovery may require disconnecting barrel power. This is stated in the firmware/specification rather than treated as a broken diagnostic.
- The obsolete stamp on the barrel-jack drawing calls for checking availability when purchasing the seven hand parts. It does not establish that the chosen part is unobtainable. Supplier-specific rotations and soldering yield cannot be certified by local CAD; retain placement confirmation and inspect the actual returned placement/stencil preview.
- Tiny factory-reflowed USB ground contacts use solid plane connections; hand-soldered connectors keep their thermal reliefs and the corrected both-face solder-mask openings. A ground stitch joins the small fan-row copper region created by rerouting.

## Electrical basis and limits

The exact P14 Pro PST manual specifies 25 kHz PWM (21–28 kHz accepted), low at most 0.8 V, sourced current at most 5 mA and open-circuit voltage at most 5.25 V. The product page lists 0 RPM below 5% PWM and recommends no more than six fans on a 2 A header. That supports a four-fan nominal load budget; it does not supply a maximum inrush waveform. [ARCTIC manual](https://support.arctic.de/en/p14-pro-pst), [ARCTIC product](https://www.arctic.de/en/P14-Pro-PST/ACFAN00314A).

MCC specifies 41.4 milliohms maximum at VGS = 1.8 V and ID = 4.3 A, at the stated 25°C datasheet condition. Applied to a 5 mA PWM load, the ohmic estimate is 0.207 mV, versus the fan's 800 mV limit. Even a deliberately severe 100× resistance sensitivity remains about 21 mV. This is substantial margin; the temperature sensitivity is not a manufacturer guarantee. The 20 V drain rating and ±8 V gate rating cover the intended signal voltages. [MCC SI2312 datasheet](https://www.mccsemi.com/pdf/Products/SI2312%28SOT-23%29.pdf).

The tach circuit uses the established common-gate translator arrangement: when the fan pulls low, the body diode initiates conduction and the channel passes the low to the source; when the fan side is high, the MCU pull-up defines its own high level. Removing USB brings the gate down and blocks a positive fan-side voltage, apart from specified leakage and capacitive transients. The signal is **not inverted**. [Nexperia/NXP AN10441](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/application-note/AN10441.pdf).

At 3.6 V, a 2.2k ±1% pull-up sources at most 1.653 mA into a low tach output. MCC's 25°C IDSS limit of 1 µA produces at most 2.222 mV across the pull-up with the fan side held high. A separate 100 µA-per-channel leakage sensitivity produces 222.2 mV relative to the logic rail and about 0.404 V on the unpowered rail through R19; it is a stress calculation, not a hot-temperature leakage specification. R19 consumes about 3.3 mA in normal operation. Negative signal faults, ESD compliance and arbitrary miswiring are not claimed as qualified.

The known output capacitance charging sensitivity from the review decreases from about 0.409 A to about **0.041 A** when C8 increases tenfold, under the same capacitance/slew assumptions. That gives more margin during charging, while staging separates motor startup from charging. Slowing the ramp does not prove fan standby current or the eFuse's transient temperature. R40 follows the explicit TI recommendation for CdVdt above 10 nF. [TI TPS2597 datasheet, sections 7.3–7.5 and package drawings](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2597.pdf).

Current reports live in `reports/review-fixes/`, `reports/simulation-b1/` and the normal top-level release reports. The existing audit checks all **54** PTH solder-mask openings. Simulation output is explicitly labelled behavioural/sensitivity analysis.

Factory placement count is **63 SMT parts**; hand work remains **7 through-hole parts / 23 joints**. Two added resistors use existing Basic part types, C8 consolidates with an existing capacitor type, and Q2–Q9 share one replacement Extended transistor type. The added part material is small, but no new assembled-board total or feeder/stock guarantee is claimed without a fresh quote.

The executed review and its frozen baseline remain unchanged. Do not apply their historical PASS results to B.1 without the new checks. Supplier review and actual first-board tests remain necessary. No order, payment, stock reservation or supplier message was made during these corrections.

Final verification: ERC and full DRC/schematic parity passed with zero violations or unconnected items. All 230 numbered physical occurrences / 211 logical pins match. The plotted U4 paste has 16 verified aperture flashes, and 1,257 positive silk draw primitives passed the width check. Both tach power-sequence sensitivity cases passed: the largest simulated tach excursion above the logic rail was approximately 0.099 V. This is behavioural evidence, not a hardware guarantee.
